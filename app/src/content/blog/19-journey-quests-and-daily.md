Learning to trade fails for a boring reason: nothing marks progress. A chart looks the same on day one and day ninety. The Journey layer exists to fix that — levels, streaks, a daily decision and a certificate at the end of it.

## The Journey

One page that answers "where am I": your level, your XP, your streak, what you have completed and what is next. It carries a dozen *try it live* links, each one dropping you into the screen a lesson describes rather than leaving you to find it.

XP is awarded for things that are actually skill:

- Finishing a lesson or a lab
- Logging a forecast **and being well calibrated when it resolves** — not for being right
- Keeping a journal note on your fills
- Completing the daily challenge

There is no XP for volume. An app that rewarded trading frequency would be teaching the single most expensive habit in the market.

## The first-flight quest

A guided run through the app's spine: your first paper trade, your first limit order, your first look at the proof ledger, your first lesson. It exists because a desk with six destinations and twenty desks is intimidating on day one, and a five-minute path through it is not.

The what's-new banner does the equivalent job for returning users: what changed in this release, in one line, with a way to read the longer story.

## The daily challenge

One 60-second macro scenario a day. A situation, a decision, and an explanation of what the decision would have cost or earned.

Sixty seconds is the design: it is short enough to do every day, and doing it every day is the entire mechanism. Streaks matter here for the same reason they matter in language apps — the habit is the product, and a single 60-second decision a day is a hundred deliberate reps in a season.

## Discover and the Town Square

**Discover** is the shorter editorial shelf: pieces about the wider crypto world, for the days when you want to read rather than trade.

The **Town Square** is the community corner inside the Academy. This blog is the long-form section next to it — see [What is DexLadder?](#/blog/what-is-dexladder) if you have arrived here first.

## The Monument

A three-dimensional tribute to the network's pseudonymous founder, sculpted from the network itself. It is the app's one piece of pure ornament, and it is not an accident: the Academy teaches that this thing was given away by someone who then walked off and never claimed the credit, and a monument makes that stick better than a paragraph does.

## The certificate

Finish the path and you can mint a certificate of what you completed and how your paper account performed, signed with your own key. It is verifiable by anyone who has the signature, which makes it a claim rather than a screenshot — and the independent auditor described in [Portfolio, the Journal and Your Record](#/blog/portfolio-journal-and-record) can check the ledger behind it without DexLadder's help.

## Why gamification, carefully

Points can teach the wrong thing very efficiently. The rules here are deliberate:

1. **Reward the process, not the outcome.** Calibration, not correctness. Notes, not profits.
2. **Never reward activity.** No XP for trades placed.
3. **Cap the loop.** One daily challenge, not an infinite feed.
4. **Everything stays on your device.** No leaderboard against strangers, no account, nothing to farm. Your streak is between you and your own machine.
